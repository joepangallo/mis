Questions 1 to 10 · choose one answer

Multiple choice

1 · Paper invoices

A company that prints and mails paper invoices for utilities has seen its volume fall forty-four percent in four years. The number of firms offering the service has not changed, two rounds of price cuts brought back no volume at all, and the utilities say their customers now take invoices by email. Which force explains it?

2 · Ceramic blocks

A dental laboratory mills crowns from ceramic blocks that fit only one manufacturer’s machines. That manufacturer raised block prices nineteen percent and set a minimum order. Moving to another system would mean replacing a $180,000 mill. Which force is this?

3 · Four wholesalers

A cleaning-products maker reaches shops through four wholesalers, who between them carry its goods to eighty-two percent of the shops that stock them. Each wholesaler reviews what it lists once a year, and one dropped two of the maker’s lines last year. Which force is strongest here?

4 · Fifteen years, then this spring

A firm has competed with the same three companies for fifteen years. This spring a company that had never worked in this county opened an office two miles away. Which reading of the situation is correct?

5 · The evidence for a rating

6 · Thirty-eight percent of the hours

A tile distributor’s crews spend thirty-eight percent of their paid hours driving, because deliveries go out in the order the orders were taken rather than by where the sites are. Which activity would a routing system improve?

7 · Which one is inbound logistics

8 · Which band is it in

9 · Where the saving lands

A contractor buys an estimating system that cuts the time to prepare a bid from nine hours to three. Bidding is done by the sales team, and the hours saved are the sales team’s own. In which activity does the saving appear?

10 · Two systems, identical payback

A firm wins work by committing to installation dates none of its competitors will promise, and it has never been the cheapest. It is offered two systems costing the same and paying back in the same time. System A cuts material cost by seven percent. System B tells each customer, in real time, whether their installation date is still on. Which does the alignment test favour?

Part three

One firm, both frameworks, one recommendation

The brief

Halloway Casework

Halloway Casework is not a real company. Every figure below is invented for this exercise.

Halloway builds fitted cabinetry to order — reception desks, laboratory benching, clinic storage — for offices, medical practices and research buildings across one region. Revenue last year was $4.9 million on costs of $4.1 million, with thirty-four staff between the shop, the install crews and a four-person office.

  • Halloway has never been the low bid. It wins by committing to installation dates on projects where a delay stops a building opening, and it says so in every proposal.
  • The laminate architects name in their specifications is made by one manufacturer, which raised its price nineteen percent this year and shortened its payment terms. Substituting another laminate needs the architect’s written approval, which has taken six weeks on average and been refused twice in the last year.
  • Halloway bids against four other shops. Bids usually land within five percent of each other, and Halloway has won about one job in five for three years running — the same share as before.
  • No new shop has opened in the region in four years. A CNC router and a spray booth together cost about $400,000 before anybody is hired.
  • Thirty-one general contractors buy from Halloway. The largest is twelve percent of revenue, and each project is bid separately.
  • Ready-made modular cabinets are now good enough for storerooms and break rooms, which is eight percent of Halloway’s revenue.
  • Twenty-two percent of install-crew hours are spent waiting for site access that was promised for a date the contractor then moved.
  • Each bid takes about nine hours to estimate, and one in five is won.

31 · Rate all five forces

Rate every one of the five competitive forces at Halloway as weak, moderate or strong. For each one give the fact from the brief that supports your rating. Finish by naming the single strongest force and saying why it beats the one you ranked second.

32 · Find two genuinely different opportunities

Using the value chain, name two activities where a system could create a real operational or customer-facing advantage for Halloway. They have to be two different activities, not one activity described twice. For each of the two, give all three of these:

  1. The activity, named in the model’s own words.
  2. The dollar figure and the driver, quoted from the exhibit — not your own paraphrase of them.
  3. One sentence saying what the weakness actually is.

33 · Write the recommendation

In 200 to 250 words, write the recommendation you would put in front of Halloway’s owner. All seven of the following have to be in it, and they have to agree with each other.

  1. The strongest force, named, with the one fact from the brief that proves it.
  2. The activity you are targeting, named in the model’s own words, with its figure from the exhibit.
  3. Exactly one initiative — not a shortlist — described concretely enough that somebody could cost it and later check whether it shipped.
  4. What kind of system it is: transaction processing, management information, decision support, customer relationship management, or another category you can name.
  5. How it would run: built and hosted by Halloway, or a cloud service — and if cloud, which service model, for a firm whose whole office is four people.
  6. The measure and the date: the number as it stands today, the number you expect instead, and when somebody should look at it.
  7. Why it fits the position Halloway has chosen. The brief says what the firm has always sold itself on.
