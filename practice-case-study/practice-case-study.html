Scenario

A training provider watching its market change underneath it

Riverside Professional Development is not a real company. It is invented for this exercise, the way the practice examples in your lecture modules are, and any organization or number in it is hypothetical.

Riverside has run in-person certification courses out of two training centers for more than a decade — OSHA and trade-license renewals, healthcare recertification hours, and small-business licensing prep. Its instructors are well regarded locally, and for years its only real competitor was the community college down the road. Over the last two years, enrollment has been flat while several online course platforms have started offering the same certifications on their own schedule, at a lower price, with a certificate issued the moment a student finishes. Front-desk staff say more callers now ask “is this available online?” before they ask about price or dates. Leadership does not want to guess its way into a technology purchase; it wants the pressure named, the real internal opportunities scanned, and one initiative recommended that could actually be costed, built, and judged afterward.

Nothing about this scenario is drawn from any real company, and nothing here should resemble any other case you have been given in this course. If any detail sounds familiar to you for reasons outside this class, that is a coincidence — treat this Riverside and only this Riverside.
6 pts

Task 1 · Name the pressure (Chapter 2 — the five competitive forces)

Apply Porter’s five competitive forces to Riverside. For each of the five, write one or two sentences saying whether it is weak, moderate, or strong right now, using evidence from the scenario or a reasonable stated assumption — not a guess with no support. Close with one sentence naming the single force doing the most damage right now, and why it beats the other four.

6 pts

Task 2 · Find the opportunities (Chapter 2 — the value chain)

Using the value chain, name at least two internal activities — primary or support — where a system could create a real operational or customer-facing advantage for Riverside. For each area: name the activity, describe the specific weakness the scenario points to (or a reasonable assumption you state), and say briefly why a system could help. Two is a floor, not a target: the second area is what proves you scanned the chain instead of stopping at the first idea you had.

8 pts

Task 3 · Recommend exactly one initiative (Chapters 1, 2 & 3)

Recommend exactly one information system for Riverside to build, drawn from one of the two areas you named above. Do not list several options and let the reader pick. Your recommendation must cover all four parts below.

  • What it does — specific enough that someone could cost it and later judge whether it shipped, not a slogan like “modernize enrollment.”
  • What kind of system it is (Chapter 1) — a transaction processing system, a management information system report, a decision support system, an executive support system, or a more specific kind such as an e-commerce, CRM, or collaboration system — and why that label fits.
  • How it would run (Chapter 3) — would Riverside build and host this itself, or use a cloud provider? If cloud, which service model fits best — infrastructure as a service, platform as a service, or software as a service — and why? Name the one infrastructure pressure from the chapter (obsolescence, rising energy costs, growing storage needs, fluctuating demand, or the need to move quickly) that most supports your choice.
  • How you would know it worked — one measure and a rough timeframe.
5 pts

Task 4 · Compare against a real company (Chapter 2)

Choose one real company you can read about — any size, any industry — that has done something with technology Riverside could learn from. In 150 to 200 words, say what the company did, what makes it worth comparing to Riverside even though the two are probably a very different size, and the one lesson that would still make sense at Riverside’s much smaller scale. A lesson that only works at the larger company’s budget or footprint is not a lesson that transfers.

0 words Aim for 150–200 words.
