Modules 1–3 Jeopardy

Fifty clues over two boards plus a Final Jeopardy capstone. The first board is the vocabulary you have to have — systems, the digital world, ethics, infrastructure. The second board is the work you have to do with it — competitive forces, the value chain, generic strategies, the cloud, and the four moves a written framework analysis asks for. Pick a category and a dollar value, decide your answer, then reveal it and grade yourself honestly.

How to play

  • Goal: clear both boards and bank as much money as you can. You choose the category and the value, the same way the real game works.
  • Every clue is a statement. Your answer is phrased as a question — “What is the value chain?” not “The value chain.”
  • Answer honestly. Nothing is typed and nothing is graded for you. Decide your answer, tap Reveal answer, then say whether you actually had it.
  • No penalty for a miss on the boards. This is a study tool, not an elimination game: right answers add the tile's value, misses just move on. Final Jeopardy is the one place a wrong answer costs you, because you chose the wager.
  • Every answer explains itself. Under each answer is a sentence saying why it is that one and not the term next to it, because the near-miss is what the exam tests.
  • The score is not the point. At the end you get a breakdown by course objective, so a weak area shows up as a named objective to reread instead of disappearing into one number.

The two boards

Round one · $200–$1,000 · what the terms mean

Round two · Double Jeopardy · $400–$2,000 · what you do with them

The last category is the written assignment, not more vocabulary Building the Recommendation asks about the shape of the framework analysis itself: how many forces you rate, how many value chain areas count, how many initiatives you recommend, how long the comparison runs, and what makes a recommendation checkable later. Those clues are worth playing even if you already know every definition on the first board.

Which course objectives this board covers

Every clue is tagged with the course objectives it serves, and the results screen reports them separately. Modules 1 to 3 do not reach all eleven, and this board does not pretend otherwise — the three it leaves alone are listed at the bottom so you know where they are covered instead.

Double Jeopardy

The first board is clear. Your score is $0. The second board is worth double, and it stops asking what the terms mean and starts asking what you do with them.

What changes

  • Values double — $400 through $2,000.
  • The clues stop being definitions. Most of them describe a situation and ask you to name what is going on, which is the form the material takes once you are the person being asked.
  • Your score carries over. Nothing from round one is lost.
