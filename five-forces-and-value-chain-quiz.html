Thirty questions, marked as you go

Every question is a situation rather than a definition, because that is the form the material takes once you are the person being asked. Choose an answer and it is marked immediately — right or wrong, all four options explain themselves, so the three you did not choose teach you something too.

How this works

  • Immediate feedback. The moment you choose, the answer is marked and every option opens up: why the right one is right, and why each wrong one is the mistake it is.
  • No going back and no penalty. Your first choice is the one that counts, which is the only way the score means anything. A wrong answer costs nothing except a note of what to reread.
  • Keyboard if you prefer it. Press 1 to 4 to choose an option, then Enter or the right arrow to move on.
  • The score is not the point. At the end you get a breakdown by topic and by course objective, plus every question you missed with its explanation, so a weak area shows up as a named thing to go back to instead of disappearing into one number.
  • Nothing is submitted or collected. Only your best percentage is remembered, in this browser, so you can see whether a second pass actually moved.

What is on it

Six topics, five questions each. The first three ask whether you can name what you are looking at; the last three ask what you would do about it, which is what the written analysis is graded on.

TopicQuestionsWhat it asks of you
Two frameworks, two different questions The five forces look outward and say where the pressure is coming from. The value chain looks inward and says which piece of your own work could answer it. Half the questions here exist because those two get answered with each other.

Which course objectives it covers

Each question is tagged with the course objectives it serves, and the results screen reports them separately. This quiz is deliberately narrow — two frameworks, not a chapter — so it claims four objectives and no more.
